Why it matters

Soil is a resource we lose silently.

Soil erosion rarely announces itself. It shows up later — as reduced yields, poor water retention and saplings that never survive their first summer.

🌊

Topsoil washes away

India loses about 5,334 million tonnes of soil every year to erosion — an average rate of roughly 16.4 tonnes per hectare per year, according to government estimates.

Source: The Hindu / Govt. of India
🌾

Land under degradation

ICAR assessments place soil loss above 10 tonnes/ha/yr on roughly 92.4 million hectares of cultivable land, while about 120.4 million hectares of the country's land is reported as degraded.

Source: PIB / ICAR
🗺

Erosion is local

In Nashik district, soil erodibility (the K factor) varies widely across the landscape — from about 0.13 to 0.53 t·ha·h/ha·MJ·mm — which means protection has to be planned location by location, not as one blanket rule.

Source: Int. J. Adv. Biochem. Res.
Our mission

Turning environmental data into real-world action 🌱

EcoGuardians aims to create a bridge between environmental observations and meaningful community action. Instead of a one-time plantation drive, the project builds a repeatable loop: observe, document, map, analyse — then act.

  • Community-driven environmental reporting
  • Location-based environmental insights
  • Data-driven decision making for plantation follow-up
  • Collaboration with environmental experts and local stakeholders
Soil Protection Erosion Control Plantation GIS Mapping Community Engagement

How the loop works

Four steps that turn a field visit into evidence.

1
Observe

Visit the site and record what the soil, vegetation and surroundings actually show.

2
Document

Capture photographs, GPS coordinates, dates and field notes for every location.

3
Map & analyse

Plot the locations and convert survey responses into charts and patterns.

4
Act

Share findings with the community and experts so plantation efforts get followed up.

Small data, big impact

Every observation adds to a clearer picture of our environment.

A single field note is not much on its own. But a hundred survey responses, three mapped locations and eighteen photographs of the same district begin to show a pattern — and patterns are what make protection measurable.
